> [!TLDR]
> - Two dossiers: the personal data processing impact assessment (Article 21) and the cross-border transfer impact assessment (Article 20); each is sent as 01 original to the specialized agency within 60 days of the first day of processing or transfer.
> - Each dossier is prepared once for the whole period of operation, updated every 06 months when something changes, or immediately in certain cases (Article 22).
> - There are exemptions and relief for small businesses; dossier contents and procedures are detailed by the Government.

This article only explains legal text, as of November 2026, and is not legal advice for any specific case. The basics of data, data subject rights and the roles of the parties are in [[Personal Data Protection Law 91/2025/QH15: basic and sensitive data, data subject rights, controllers and processors]].

## Introduction

Law 91/2025/QH15 (passed by the National Assembly on 26 June 2025, effective 1 January 2026) creates two dossiers that organizations processing personal data need to know: the personal data processing impact assessment dossier and the cross-border personal data transfer impact assessment dossier. This is the part most often misunderstood, because it has two time limits, many exceptions, and many details delegated to the Government. This article walks through each article so you know what the law says and where you must consult implementing documents.

## Personal data processing impact assessment (Article 2, clause 12; Article 21)

The law defines this as analyzing and assessing risks that may arise during processing so that risk-mitigation measures can be applied. Under Article 21:

- The controller, or controller-processor, **prepares and keeps** the dossier and **sends 01 original** to the specialized personal data protection agency within **60 days of the first day of processing**.
- The dossier is prepared **once** for the whole period of operation and updated under Article 22.
- The processor prepares and keeps its own dossier under its agreement with the controller.
- If the dossier is incomplete or incorrect, the specialized agency requires it to be completed.
- When the content already sent changes, it must be updated and supplemented.
- Competent state agencies do not have to follow this provision.

The dossier components, conditions, order and procedures are detailed by the Government (clause 7). So if you need to know which sections the dossier must contain, read the implementing decree rather than guessing from the law.

## Cross-border transfer impact assessment (Article 20)

Article 20, clause 1 lists three cases treated as cross-border transfer:

1. Moving data stored in Vietnam to a storage system located outside Vietnam.
2. An agency, organization or individual in Vietnam transferring data to an organization or individual abroad.
3. An agency, organization or individual in Vietnam or abroad using a **platform located outside Vietnam** to process data collected in Vietnam.

The third case deserves attention from technical staff: using a platform located abroad to process data collected in Vietnam falls within the definition, not only "sending a file abroad".

The cross-border transfer dossier is prepared and one original sent to the specialized agency within **60 days of the first day of transfer**, prepared **once** for the whole period of operation and updated under Article 22. The specialized agency may inspect periodically no more than once a year, or inspect without notice when it finds a violation or when a data leak or loss occurs; it may also decide to require a transfer to stop when it finds the data being used in activities that could harm national defense or security.

The cases that do **not** have to carry out this assessment (clause 6) are: transfers by competent state agencies; an agency or organization storing its own employees' personal data on a cloud service; a data subject transferring their own data abroad; and other cases prescribed by the Government.

Article 5, clause 4 also reduces duplication: an organization that has completed both impact assessments under this Law does not have to carry out a data risk assessment or cross-border transfer impact assessment under data law.

## Updating the dossiers (Article 22)

Both dossiers are updated **every 06 months when there is a change**, or **immediately** when:

- the organization is restructured, ceases operation, dissolves or goes bankrupt;
- information about the provider of personal data protection services changes;
- a business line or service related to personal data processing registered in the dossier arises or changes.

Updates are made on the national personal data protection portal or at the specialized agency. The specialized agency is part of the Ministry of Public Security (Article 33, clause 1, point a).

## Exemptions and transition

Article 38 lets small businesses and start-ups **choose** whether to comply with Articles 21 and 22 and Article 33, clause 2 for **05 years** from the date the law takes effect; household businesses and micro-enterprises do not have to comply with these provisions. Both groups have an exception: if they provide personal data processing services, directly process sensitive data, or process data of a large number of data subjects, they must still comply. The Government details these clauses, so what "large number" means in practice must be checked in implementing documents.

Article 39 is the transitional provision: dossiers under Decree 13/2023/NĐ-CP (of 17 April 2023) already received by the specialized agency before the law took effect continue to be used and need not be prepared again; later updates follow the new Law. Processing already consented to or agreed under the old decree may also continue without asking again.

## Consequences of violations

Article 8, clause 4 sets the maximum fine for an organization violating cross-border transfer rules at 05% of the previous year's revenue; if there is no revenue, or the revenue-based amount is lower than VND 3 billion, the VND 3 billion level applies (clause 5). This describes how the statutory maximum is calculated, not a forecast of an actual fine in any case.

## Summary

- Two dossiers, two 60-day deadlines, each prepared once and updated under Article 22.
- Using a platform outside Vietnam to process data collected in Vietnam is also a cross-border transfer.
- Check the exceptions in Article 20, clause 6 and Article 38; for dossier contents, see the implementing decree.

The technical work to prepare for these dossiers is split into a separate article at the end of the series, covering data classification, encryption, access logs, deletion and incident notification.
