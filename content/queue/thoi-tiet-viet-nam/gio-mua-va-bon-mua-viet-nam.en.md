> [!TLDR]
> - A monsoon is a wind that changes direction with the seasons; Vietnam gets the northeast monsoon (cooler, drier season) and the southwest monsoon (hot, rainy season).
> - The north is farther from the equator, feels the cold spells of the northeast monsoon and has four clear seasons; the south is closer to the equator, hot all year, and splits into a wet season and a dry season.
> - The seasons are not caused by the Earth being nearer to or farther from the Sun, but by the tilt of its axis.

## Introduction

If you have ever travelled from Hanoi in the twelfth lunar month back to Ho Chi Minh City, you will have noticed something odd right away: the same country, the same day, yet in one place you need a warm jacket and in the other you are still in a T-shirt. The reason lies in two words, "monsoon", and in the way Vietnam stretches from north to south. This article explains from the beginning, in plain words, why the weather here changes with the seasons and why the north differs from the south.

## Where do the seasons come from?

Many people think summer is hot because the Earth is closer to the Sun then. That is not true. Seasons exist because the Earth's rotation axis is tilted relative to the plane of its orbit (by about 23.4 degrees). When one half of the Earth leans toward the Sun, that half receives more direct light and longer days, so it is warm; the other half is in its cold season. The two hemispheres have opposite seasons: when it is summer in Vietnam, it is winter in Australia. If you would like to see the Earth in the wider picture, read [[The eight planets of the Solar System: size, distance and why Pluto became a dwarf planet]].

The tilt tells us why temperatures rise and fall through the year. Whether it rains a lot or a little, and where the wind comes from, is mostly decided by the monsoon.

## What is a monsoon?

A monsoon is a wind system that reverses direction almost completely between the two halves of the year. The basic cause is that land and sea heat up and cool down at different speeds. In winter the Asian continent cools faster than the sea, and heavy cold air gathers into a high-pressure area and flows out toward the sea. In summer the land warms faster, warm air rises, a low-pressure area forms, and it pulls moist air from the ocean into the continent.

Vietnam lies on the eastern edge of the Asian continent, facing the East Sea (South China Sea), so it is affected by both flows.

### The northeast monsoon

The northeast monsoon blows into Vietnam mainly from about November to March or April. It carries cold air down from a high-pressure area in the north of Asia. Each such cold surge is often called a "northeast monsoon wave" or a "strengthened cold air mass", and it is the reason the north has some days of severe cold in winter. The farther south it goes, the weaker the cold air becomes, because it travels a long way and is gradually modified, so the south barely notices it.

### The southwest monsoon

The southwest monsoon is strongest from about May or June to September or October. It brings plenty of water vapour from the Indian Ocean and the Gulf of Thailand, causing heavy rain in the South, the Central Highlands and many other places. When the southwest wind crosses the Truong Son range and descends the eastern slope, the air becomes dry and hot, producing the hot, dry spells in central Vietnam that people call the "Laos wind" or southwest foehn wind.

## Why does the north have four seasons?

The north lies roughly between 20 and 23 degrees north, farther from the equator and close to the cold region further north, so two things decide its weather: the angle of the Sun's rays and the cold air masses. The result is:

- **Spring** (about February to April): cool, high humidity, frequent drizzle.
- **Summer** (about May to August): hot and humid, a lot of rain, sometimes storms.
- **Autumn** (about September to November): mild and pleasant, drier skies.
- **Winter** (about December to January or February): cold, less rain, with cold spells.

These months are only approximate and may shift by a few weeks each year. What matters is that the temperature difference between the hot and cold seasons here is large enough for us to feel four distinct periods.

## Why does the south only have wet and dry seasons?

The south lies closer to the equator (roughly 8 to 11 degrees north), so the Sun shines fairly directly almost all year and temperatures barely differ across the year. Cold air from the north has almost always faded by the time it arrives. So the biggest change through the year is not hot versus cold but rain versus no rain:

- **Wet season** (about May to November): the southwest monsoon brings moisture, rain usually comes in the late afternoon or evening and clears quickly.
- **Dry season** (about December to April): little rain, lots of sun, with the sultriest days around March and April.

Central Vietnam has its own character: in many places the rain peaks in the last months of the year (about September to December), partly because of storms and partly because the northeast monsoon meets the Truong Son range. That is why the rainy months in Hue or Da Nang differ from those in Can Tho.

## An example to picture it

Imagine you have a friend in Hanoi and another in Can Tho. In January the friend in Hanoi wears a coat, while the one in Can Tho still wears short sleeves under a sunny sky. In July both are hot, but the one in Hanoi gets summer showers and storms, while the one in Can Tho gets an afternoon downpour almost every day. It is the same monsoon system, but different locations give different results.

## Summary

- Seasons come from the tilt of the Earth's axis, not from its distance to the Sun.
- The northeast monsoon brings cooler, drier air; the southwest monsoon brings moisture and rain.
- The north is far from the equator and so has four seasons; the south is close to it and only distinguishes wet from dry; the centre has its own rainfall pattern.
