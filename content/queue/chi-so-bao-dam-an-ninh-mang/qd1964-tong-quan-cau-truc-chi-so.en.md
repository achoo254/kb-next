> [!TLDR]
> - Decision 1964/QĐ-TTg (signed 12 October 2026) creates the Cybersecurity Assurance Index, a uniform, quantitative assessment for ministries, agencies and localities.
> - The Index has 3 groups of criteria and 29 component criteria: Group I (2 criteria), Group II (18 criteria) and Group III (9 criteria, not counted in the score).
> - The central basis for assessment is the standard TCVN 14423:2026; the scales in the annexes are self-assessment scales for a trial period.

*This article only explains the document and is not legal advice for any specific case. Content is current as of November 2026.*

## Introduction

When people talk about the cybersecurity of an organization, a common question is "so where do we stand?". Decision No. 1964/QĐ-TTg of 12 October 2026 of the Prime Minister answers with a number: the **Cybersecurity Assurance Index**. This article reads Annexes I and II of the decision to answer three questions: what the Index is for, which parts it is divided into, and which parts actually count toward the score. The next two articles in this planet go deeper into scoring and into preparing evidence.

A note on scope: this article relies only on the annexes of the decision. Article numbers for Articles 1 to 4 are not cited here; open the official text (link under References) when you need the exact scope and responsibilities for organizing implementation.

## What the Index is for

According to Annex I, the Index is built to assess the level of cybersecurity assurance of ministries, agencies and localities in a **uniform, objective and quantitative** way. The results are used to monitor, compare and rank, to detect weaknesses and risks early, and to support state management, inspection, supervision and policy making.

Annex I states several principles that matter to technical staff:

- **Verifiable.** Assessment must be checkable against records, documents, actual implementation results and monitoring data; the text says explicitly that formal assessment or self-declaration without grounds should be limited.
- **Tied to a standard.** Compliance with the standard TCVN 14423:2026 (Cybersecurity - Information systems - Basic requirements) is the central basis for assessment.
- **Broad.** The Index reflects governance, technology, operations, monitoring, incident response, data protection, resources and the responsibility of the head of the organization.
- **Open and updatable.** The Index can be reviewed and adjusted periodically as technology and the cybersecurity situation change.

The method is **scoring**: each requirement gets a level of compliance based on the degree of actual implementation, the completeness of records and evidence, the degree of maintenance and effectiveness, and the results of inspection, monitoring or independent assessment. Results are aggregated by criteria group and then converted into a ranking level.

## Structure: 3 groups, 29 criteria

Annex I states that the Index consists of **3 groups of criteria and 29 component criteria**, listed in Annex II. Only the first two groups are used to calculate the score.

### Group I: general assessment (20%)

Applies to every information system and every system owner, with 2 criteria:

- I.1 Compliance with the level-proposal dossier;
- I.2 Compliance with the cybersecurity assurance plan for the level.

This group checks the "compliant with rules" part: has the system been classified and approved at a level, and are the measures in the approved plan fully implemented? How a level is determined depends on cybersecurity law; if you need that part, see [[The five security levels under Decree 331/2026/NĐ-CP: criteria, personal-data thresholds and the highest-level rule]].

### Group II: specific assessment (80%)

Applies to information systems at **level 3 or higher** and their owners, with 18 criteria measuring real capability. The text describes this as the group that reflects protection capability on the ground in a comprehensive way. The criteria cover:

- cybersecurity risk management;
- management of hardware, software and information assets;
- secure configuration of hardware and software;
- account and access management;
- vulnerability management;
- cybersecurity log management;
- protection of web browsers and email services;
- malware prevention;
- data backup and recovery;
- network infrastructure management;
- cybersecurity defense monitoring;
- personnel who operate, administer and protect security;
- supplier management for products and services;
- secure application development;
- cybersecurity incident response governance;
- cybersecurity inspection management.

Each criterion has a weight (a number from 7 to 10 in the table). A footnote to the table says weights only show relative importance within the same group and are not compared directly between two different groups.

Reading this list, infrastructure people will see almost every familiar operations task: inventorying servers, patching, managing accounts, collecting logs, backing up, monitoring. The Index is, in effect, a way of "weighing" that work.

### Group III: for direction and management (0%)

Group III has 9 statistical criteria, applies to every system, but is **not used to calculate the Index** (weight 0%). These are figures such as the rate of risk-treatment measures done on time, the rate of incidents handled in time, the number of serious incidents, the number of citizens' personal-data records leaked, the amount of data leaked, lost or encrypted, and financial losses relative to the cybersecurity budget. They help leaders follow the situation rather than rank anyone.

## What to remember when reading the scales

Annex III says clearly that the content and levels in the annexes are **self-assessment scales for the trial implementation period**, not a mandatory technical regulation or standard applied universally. The system owner assesses and scores itself. That does not mean doing it casually: the scoring method requires explanation and evidence for each level, as the next article shows in [[Scoring the Cybersecurity Assurance Index: Levels 0 to 3, the aggregation formula and four rating levels]].

## Summary

- The Cybersecurity Assurance Index is a uniform measuring tool, based on TCVN 14423:2026 and verifiable evidence.
- The 29 criteria fall into three groups; only Group I (2 criteria) and Group II (18 criteria, for systems at level 3 or higher) are scored, while Group III (9 criteria) is only for monitoring.
- Most criteria are the familiar tasks of secure operations, so preparing well for the Index also means improving real operations.
