> [!TLDR]
> - In late 938, Ngô Quyền destroyed the Southern Han fleet on the Bạch Đằng River.
> - The key was a field of stakes driven into the riverbed, using the rise and fall of the tide.
> - The victory opened the way to lasting self-rule; in 939 Ngô Quyền proclaimed himself king.

## Introduction

Some battles decide an entire era. The Battle of Bạch Đằng in late 938 is one of them: it closed the period of Chinese domination that had lasted about a thousand years and laid the groundwork for the later independent dynasties. This article retells the background, the course and the meaning of the battle, based on Wikipedia.

## Background

In the early 10th century, the land of the Vietnamese was run by a self-governing authority called Tĩnh Hải quân (the Jinghai Circuit), though nominally still within the sphere of the northern dynasties. After its leader Dương Đình Nghệ was killed, Ngô Quyền, one of his generals, rose against the new man in power. The other side asked the state of Southern Han for help, and Southern Han sent a naval force by sea, commanded by Prince Lưu Hoằng Tháo.

Ngô Quyền knew the Southern Han army was strong at sea, with large warships and many men. Fighting head-on on the river would be a disadvantage. So he chose to fight with terrain and timing.

## The course of the battle

According to historical sources, Ngô Quyền had large stakes with sharpened iron tips driven into the bed of the Bạch Đằng River, in the estuary area near today's Hạ Long Bay. The key point was the tide:

1. **At high tide**, the rows of stakes were hidden under the water, and the enemy boats could not see them.
2. The Vietnamese sent small boats to provoke the enemy, then pretended to retreat to **lure** the Southern Han boats deep into the river.
3. **At low tide**, the sharpened stakes were exposed. The enemy's large boats were trapped or pierced, while the Vietnamese counterattacked.

As a result, the Southern Han fleet was destroyed and Lưu Hoằng Tháo was killed in action. It is a classic example of using geographical knowledge and the laws of nature to make up for a difference in strength.

## Why the battle matters

### Ending Chinese domination

After the victory, Southern Han no longer had the strength or the will to return. In the spring of 939, Ngô Quyền proclaimed himself king and chose Cổ Loa as the capital. Many historians see this as the moment Vietnamese history began to stand on its own feet.

### A lesson in strategy

The Battle of Bạch Đằng shows three principles that anyone can understand:

- **Know yourself and know the opponent:** pick the opponent's strength and make it useless.
- **Use the environment:** the tide was the greatest "ally".
- **Prepare in advance and strike at the right time:** the stakes were set beforehand, waiting only for the moment the water fell.

## Bạch Đằng appears many times in the chronicles

The name Bạch Đằng is tied to other victories as well. In 981, Lê Hoàn defeated the Song army in this region, and in 1288 the Trần forces used the same stake technique to block the Mongol–Yuan fleet. Beyond those, the victory of 938 is still often mentioned as the opening of that long chain. Note that the later battles are mentioned here only for comparison; the details of each have their own entries in the chronicles and on Wikipedia.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"There was only one Battle of Bạch Đằng":** in fact several battles bear this name, so pay attention to the year when reading history.
- **"The stakes were driven after the enemy arrived":** the sources describe the stakes as prepared in advance, based on calculations about the tide.
- **"The victory was luck":** the success came from long observation of the water and exact coordination of the forces.

## Later traces

Ngô Quyền is remembered by the Vietnamese as the founder of independence after the period of Chinese rule. Although the Ngô dynasty did not last long, it paved the way for the Đinh, the Early Lê and then the Lý, dynasties that moved the capital to a new place. The story of that capital is told in [[Thăng Long, the dragon capital: from the 1010 move to today's Imperial Citadel]].

## Summary

The Battle of Bạch Đằng in 938 is memorable not only for the victory but for how it was won: using the tide and hidden stakes to turn the opponent's strength into a weakness. From then on the Vietnamese entered an era of self-rule, and the name of the Bạch Đằng River became a symbol of military wisdom.
