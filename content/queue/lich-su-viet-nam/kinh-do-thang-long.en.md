> [!TLDR]
> - In 1010, Lý Công Uẩn moved the capital from Hoa Lư to the Đại La area and named it Thăng Long, meaning "ascending dragon".
> - The capital went by several names: Thăng Long, Đông Đô, Đông Kinh, and from 1831 Hà Nội.
> - The central sector of the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010.

## Introduction

If you visit Hanoi and stand in the middle of the Imperial Citadel area, you are standing on land that was a centre of power for more than a thousand years. The name "Thăng Long" sounds like a blessing: a dragon ascending. This article tells that story briefly: why the Lý dynasty moved the capital, how the capital was renamed, and what remains today.

## From Hoa Lư to Đại La

Before 1010, the capital of the Đinh and Early Lê dynasties was at Hoa Lư, a rugged limestone region, good for defence but cramped for growth. Lý Công Uẩn, who founded the Lý dynasty, decided to move the capital to the Đại La area by the Red River, with wide, flat land convenient for river transport and trade.

The famous text tied to this decision is the **Edict on the Transfer of the Capital** (Chiếu dời đô). According to tradition, when his boat arrived here the king saw a golden dragon rise up, so he named the capital **Thăng Long**. That is the traditional explanation of the name; historically, what is certain is that construction of the palaces began in 1010 and was completed in early 1011.

## The names of the capital

The city changed its name several times over the centuries:

| Period | Name |
| --- | --- |
| From 1010 | Thăng Long |
| Under the Hồ dynasty | Đông Đô (when the main capital moved to Tây Đô in Thanh Hóa) |
| From 1428, under Lê Lợi | Đông Kinh |
| From 1831, under Minh Mạng | Hà Nội |

Note that the name "Hà Nội" means "inside the rivers", referring to its position among the rivers. In 1802, the Nguyễn dynasty moved the capital to Huế, so from then on Thăng Long was no longer the political centre of the whole country.

## The Imperial Citadel today

The Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long is a complex of historic structures in the centre of Hanoi. Construction began in 1010 under Lý Thái Tổ. Later dynasties kept expanding and rebuilding it; in 1805 Emperor Gia Long had the old citadel rebuilt, but most of that citadel was later demolished, leaving only the North Gate and the Flag Tower. For this reason the place is like a book with many layers: many periods lie on top of each other.

On 31 July 2010, the central sector of the Citadel, named "Central Sector of the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long – Hanoi", was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List. Archaeological excavation here has found traces of many periods, from Lý palaces to later layers of architecture.

## A quick visit guide

If you want to explore, you can follow this order:

1. **Goal:** understand the historical layers of a place.
2. **Preparation:** read a few lines of introduction beforehand, and bring water and a hat.
3. **Steps:** start at the archaeological area to see the older foundation layers, then move on to the structures that remain above ground.
4. **Check:** ask yourself, "which dynasty does each layer belong to?"
5. **Common mistakes:** treating everything as belonging to one era; in fact each part belongs to a different period.

## Why Thăng Long matters

- **Location:** close to the Red River, convenient for travel and trade.
- **Symbol:** the seat of power for many dynasties: the Lý, Trần, Lê and later periods.
- **Heritage:** what archaeologists found helps us understand court life, architecture and the building techniques of the past.

## Summary

From the decision to move the capital in 1010 to the UNESCO inscription in 2010, Thăng Long is an example of how a good location, a wise choice and generations of care created a living centre across more than a thousand years. When reading history, remember that names may change, but the land remains a witness.
