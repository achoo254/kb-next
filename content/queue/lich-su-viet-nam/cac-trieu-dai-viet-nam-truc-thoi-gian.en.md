> [!TLDR]
> - Independent feudal rule by the Vietnamese began with the Ngô dynasty (939), after almost a thousand years of Chinese rule.
> - The main dynasties follow one another: Ngô, Đinh, Early Lê, Lý, Trần, Hồ, Lê sơ, Mạc, Restored Lê, Tây Sơn, Nguyễn.
> - The Nguyễn dynasty (1802–1945) was the last feudal dynasty, with its capital at Huế.

## Introduction

Learning history by memorising dynasties one by one is confusing: there are many kings, many reign names and many dates. An easier way is to look at everything on **one timeline** and attach to each dynasty the one thing most worth remembering. This article draws that timeline, from the moment the Vietnamese regained self-rule in the 10th century to the end of the monarchy in 1945. The dates follow Wikipedia; where sources differ by a few years, the article uses a general wording so that it stays correct.

## Before the Ngô: a thousand years of Chinese rule

Before the independent dynasties, the land of the Vietnamese was ruled by dynasties from the north for a very long time, usually called the period of Chinese domination, lasting about a thousand years from 111 BCE. There were many uprisings in that time, the most famous being the Trưng Sisters' revolt in the year 40. In the end, Ngô Quyền's victory on the Bạch Đằng River in 938 opened the era of self-rule; that story is told separately in [[The Battle of Bạch Đằng in 938: Ngô Quyền, the hidden stakes and the road to independence]].

## The dynasties on the timeline

### 10th century: building independence

- **Ngô dynasty (939–965):** Ngô Quyền proclaimed himself king in 939 and set his capital at Cổ Loa. After his death the country fell into disorder, known in history as the chaos of the Twelve Warlords.
- **Đinh dynasty (968–980):** Đinh Bộ Lĩnh put down the disorder, became emperor, named the country Đại Cồ Việt and set the capital at Hoa Lư.
- **Early Lê dynasty (980–1009):** Lê Hoàn took the throne and defeated an invasion by the Song dynasty in 981.

### 11th–14th centuries: stability and prosperity

- **Lý dynasty (1009–1225):** Lý Công Uẩn took the throne in 1009 and moved the capital to Thăng Long in 1010 (see [[Thăng Long, the dragon capital: from the 1010 move to today's Imperial Citadel]]). In 1054 the country's name was changed to Đại Việt.
- **Trần dynasty (1225–1400):** famous for three resistance wars against the Mongol–Yuan forces, in 1258, 1285 and 1288.

### 15th–16th centuries: upheaval and recovery

- **Hồ dynasty (1400–1407):** Hồ Quý Ly founded a short-lived dynasty with the country name Đại Ngu.
- **Ming rule (1407–1427):** the Ming dynasty ruled for almost two decades. Lê Lợi rose up at Lam Sơn and won in 1427.
- **Lê sơ (Early Lê) dynasty (1428–1527):** Lê Lợi took the throne in 1428, opening a long period of stability.
- **Mạc dynasty (1527–1592):** Mạc Đăng Dung founded the dynasty while the Lê family was restored elsewhere.

### 16th–18th centuries: Lê kings, Trịnh lords and Nguyễn lords

- **Restored Lê dynasty (1533–1789):** the Lê kings stayed on the throne, but real power lay with the Trịnh lords in the north. In the south, the Nguyễn lords governed the region called Đàng Trong. The two sides fought several times in the 17th century.
- **Tây Sơn dynasty (1778–1802):** the Tây Sơn brothers rose up; Nguyễn Huệ became emperor under the reign name Quang Trung in 1788 and defeated the Qing army in early 1789.

### 19th–20th centuries: the Nguyễn

- **Nguyễn dynasty (1802–1945):** Nguyễn Ánh took the throne in 1802 under the reign name Gia Long, with the capital at Huế. In the second half of the 19th century, French colonial power was imposed step by step. In August 1945, Emperor Bảo Đại abdicated, ending the monarchy in Vietnam.

## A quick way to remember

You can split the timeline into four "stages":

1. **Laying the foundation (939–1009):** Ngô, Đinh, Early Lê.
2. **Prosperity (1009–1400):** Lý and Trần, two dynasties tied to Thăng Long and the resistance wars.
3. **Movement (1400–1788):** Hồ, Ming rule, Lê sơ, Mạc, Restored Lê.
4. **Unification and the end (1788–1945):** Tây Sơn, then Nguyễn.

## The Nguyễn and the legacy in Huế

Because the Nguyễn were the last dynasty, much of what they left is still visible in daily life: the royal architecture of Huế, the tombs, and even court music. To imagine the atmosphere of the court at that time, you can read [[Nhã nhạc, Vietnamese court music of Huế: the imperial music recognised by UNESCO]], about the music that once sounded at the grand ceremonies of the Nguyễn court.

## Example: reading a date

Suppose you come across the number "1010". Ask three questions: which stage is it in, which dynasty, and what event? The answer: the stage of prosperity, the Lý dynasty, the move of the capital to Thăng Long. With this habit you will be less likely to get confused by an unfamiliar date.

## Notes on reading dates

- The start and end years of a dynasty are sometimes counted differently depending on the reference point (taking the throne or proclaiming kingship, losing the throne or being overthrown).
- In some periods two dynasties existed side by side, such as the Restored Lê and the Mạc, so the year ranges can overlap.
- The country's name also changed several times: Đại Cồ Việt, Đại Việt, Đại Ngu, then Việt Nam under the Nguyễn.

## Summary

From 939 to 1945, feudal Vietnamese history spans more than a thousand years of dynasties following one another, each with its own mark. When you see them on one timeline, the rhythm becomes clear: the building of the foundation, the prosperous age, the turbulent age, and finally the end of the monarchy. The next articles in this series go deeper into two highlights: the Bạch Đằng battle at the beginning and Thăng Long, the longest-standing capital.
