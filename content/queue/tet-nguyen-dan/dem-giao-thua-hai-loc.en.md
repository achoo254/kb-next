> [!TLDR]
> - Giao thừa is the moment of passage from the old year to the new, with midnight the most important time.
> - There are two offerings: one outdoors to welcome the gods and one indoors to invite the ancestors.
> - Picking a "lộc" twig at temples is a northern custom; the Central region does not have it.
> - Keep the rites simple, and respect the trees and the people around you.

## Introduction: the moment between two years

There is a moment in the year that the whole family often experiences together: New Year's Eve, called Giao thừa. It is when the old year closes and the new year begins. According to Wikipedia, Giao thừa falls between the Hour of the Pig (Hợi) on the 30th (or 29th) of the twelfth lunar month and the Hour of the Rat (Tý) on the first day; midnight is regarded as the most important moment. Many localities set off fireworks for about 10 to 15 minutes in open spaces.

This article covers three things often tied to New Year's Eve: the offerings, temple visits and picking lucky twigs. After that night comes the first morning of the year with the customs described in [[First-footing, lucky money and the first day of Tết: the customs that open the new year]].

## The outdoor Giao thừa offering

According to Wikipedia, the outdoor altar is set in front of the main door to welcome the Heavenly Soldiers (the twelve Executive Officials) who patrol the human world. By legend, the gods judge the sincerity of the household. The listed offerings include a boar's head or a chicken, bánh chưng (square sticky rice cake), bánh giầy (round sticky rice cake), candied fruit, betel and areca, fruit, wine or water, and votive paper.

This rite is also called "trừ tịch", from the idea of driving out the evil spirits of the old year. The arrangement and the prayers vary by family; what is common is careful preparation and sincerity.

## The Giao thừa offering indoors

Besides the outdoor rite, many families make an offering indoors at the moment of Giao thừa to ask the ancestors for their blessing, with a savory feast tray. According to Wikipedia, before inviting the ancestors to celebrate Tết, the head of the household prays to the Earth God (Thổ công), the spirit who governs the home, to ask permission. It is a way of showing respect in order: greet the master of the house first, then invite the ancestors.

For young people preparing it by themselves for the first time, the easiest way is to ask the elders in the family about their own custom. Each home may differ in the tray and the prayer; there is no single pattern to follow.

## Visiting a temple on New Year's Eve

Many people choose to spend the time around the turn of the year at a pagoda or temple, wishing for a peaceful year. The Wikipedia article on Tết mentions worship at pagodas or temples in its section on setting out and picking lucky twigs, without a separate section. So this article states only what is certain: it is a common habit, not an obligation, and each person can do it in their own way.

A few general tips for the trip: dress modestly, keep order, follow the paths and listen to the instructions of the place's management. New Year's Eve is usually very crowded, so pay attention to safety, especially when going with elderly people and small children.

## Picking "lộc": a northern custom

After worship, according to Wikipedia, northerners usually break off a "lộc" twig (from a banyan, bodhi or fig tree) and bring it home to place on the altar for luck. The word "lộc" means good fortune, like young buds in early spring. The Central region has no custom of picking lộc at the start of the year, so the trees at temples stay green all spring.

Wikipedia also notes some opposing views today: some worry that a spirit may follow the twig home, some worry that picking harms city trees, and in some places there are scuffles as people scramble for lộc. So many people choose a gentler way: take a twig that has already fallen, or simply wish with sincerity without breaking the tree.

## New Year's Eve at home

If you stay home, New Year's Eve can still be very meaningful. Suggestions:

- Prepare a simple offering tray, enough to show sincerity.
- Gather as a family and watch fireworks on television or outdoors if available.
- Exchange good wishes right as the new year begins.
- Rest sensibly so that you can receive guests and pay New Year visits on the first morning.

## Common mistakes

- Going to the temple too late or at the most crowded time without preparation, and getting jostled.
- Breaking off a whole large branch when picking lộc, which harms the tree.
- Setting off firecrackers against the rules; follow the local safety regulations.
- Thinking only one way of making offerings is correct; each region and each home differs.

## Summary

Giao thừa is a solemn and warm moment of passage: an outdoor offering to welcome the gods, an indoor offering to invite the ancestors, a temple visit if you wish, and a gentle picking of lộc if you follow the northern custom. Keeping sincerity and respecting the people around you matters more than a lavish tray. The last article in the series compares the Tết customs of the three regions.
