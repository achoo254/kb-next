> [!TLDR]
> - The Solar System has eight planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.
> - The four inner planets are small and rocky; the four outer planets are giants.
> - In 2006 Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet because it has not "cleared" its orbit.

## Introduction

Look up at the night sky and you will see a few bright "stars" that do not twinkle. Often these are planets, other worlds that orbit the Sun together with Earth. This article introduces the eight planets from the inside out, talks about their size and distance, and then tells the story of why Pluto is no longer on the list. The figures below are rounded values, checked against NASA and Wikipedia.

## What the Solar System contains

At the centre is the Sun, a star holding more than 99% of the whole system's mass. Around it are eight planets, natural satellites (such as Earth's Moon), dwarf planets, millions of asteroids and comets. Between Mars and Jupiter lies a region full of small bodies called the asteroid belt.

To measure such large distances, astronomers use the **astronomical unit (AU)**: the average distance from Earth to the Sun, roughly 149.6 million km. Light takes about 8 minutes to travel that distance.

### The four rocky planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars have solid surfaces, are small and lie close to the Sun. Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Venus has a thick atmosphere that traps heat strongly. Mars is smaller than Earth and looks orange-red because its surface contains a lot of iron oxide.

### The four giant planets

Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter is the largest planet: its diameter is more than ten times Earth's. Saturn is famous for its brilliant ring system. Uranus and Neptune are smaller and rich in substances such as water, ammonia and methane, so they are called "ice giants"; being the farthest away, they are also the coldest planets.

## Why Pluto was reclassified

Pluto was discovered in 1930 by Clyde Tombaugh and for several decades was considered the ninth planet. But from the 1990s astronomers found many other icy bodies beyond Neptune, some with a mass similar to Pluto's, for example Eris. The question became: if Pluto is a planet, what about those objects?

In August 2006 the International Astronomical Union (IAU) met in Prague and defined what a planet is. A body is a planet when it meets all three conditions:

1. It orbits the Sun.
2. It has enough mass for its own gravity to pull it into a nearly round shape.
3. It has "cleared" other objects from the neighbourhood of its orbit.

Pluto meets the first two conditions but not the third, because its orbit shares space with many other icy bodies. It was therefore placed in a new group, the **dwarf planets**, together with Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake. Pluto's diameter is about 2,400 km, smaller than our Moon.

It is important to understand this correctly: Pluto itself did not change. Only the way people classify it did. Science revises definitions as understanding grows, and that is a strength, not a weakness.

## Summary

The Solar System has eight planets: four rocky ones inside and four giants outside. The distances between them are so large that we measure them in astronomical units. Pluto has been a dwarf planet since 2006 because of the IAU's new definition. The planet closest to us still has a familiar companion, the Moon, and the article [[The Moon and its phases: why it waxes and wanes, and how it relates to the lunar calendar]] tells more about it.
