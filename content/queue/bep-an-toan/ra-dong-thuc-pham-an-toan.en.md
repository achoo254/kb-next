> [!TLDR]
> - There are three safe ways to thaw: in the fridge, in cold water in a sealed bag, or in the microwave followed by cooking at once.
> - Never thaw at room temperature on the counter: the outside warms up while the core is still frozen.
> - Food thawed in the fridge can be refrozen; with the other two methods you should cook it straight away.

## Goal

After this article you will know how to pick the right thawing method for the time you have, why you should not leave meat out overnight, and when it is acceptable to refreeze food.

## Why thawing needs care

Freezing makes bacteria stop multiplying but does not destroy them. When food starts to soften, the outer layer warms faster than the core. If a piece of meat sits on the counter for half a day, the outside may have been at room temperature for many hours, exactly the range where bacteria multiply fastest, while the middle is still icy. The outside looks "already soft" but has in fact been in the unsafe zone for a long time. That is why WHO and food safety agencies all advise against thawing at room temperature.

This follows on from storage in the fridge: the fridge is both the place to store food and the best place to thaw it.

## Preparation

- A tray or plate with edges to catch the liquid that runs out.
- A leak-proof sealed bag or a container with a lid.
- A food thermometer if you have one, to check the temperature when cooking.
- A plan made a day ahead: the best method needs time.

## Steps

### Method 1: Thaw in the fridge (the safest)

1. Put the food in a container or on a plate with edges and place it on the **lowest shelf** so liquid cannot drip onto other food.
2. Leave it overnight or longer. The bigger the piece, the longer it takes: a small tray of minced meat usually needs one night, while a whole chicken can take more than a day.
3. Once soft, the food can stay in the fridge for another day or two before cooking.

The big advantage: the temperature stays low, so bacteria get no chance to multiply, and if your plans change you can refreeze without cooking first (quality may drop slightly because of water loss).

### Method 2: Cold-water bath (when you need it faster)

1. Put the food in a **leak-proof sealed bag**. If the bag is open, the food absorbs water and bacteria from the water.
2. Submerge the bag in a bowl of **cold** water (not hot or lukewarm water).
3. Change to fresh cold water about every 30 minutes so the water does not warm up.
4. When soft, cook it right away.

This usually takes a few hours for a medium-sized piece of meat, much faster than the fridge.

### Method 3: Microwave (the fastest)

1. Use the defrost setting according to your oven's instructions, with the food on a microwave-safe plate.
2. Turn or stir it halfway through.
3. **Cook straight after defrosting**, because some spots may already have begun to warm up and cook in places.

### The method to avoid: leaving it on the counter or soaking in hot water

Do not leave meat, fish or chicken on the kitchen counter or soak it in hot water "to speed things up". The outer layer stays warm too long before the core softens. If you are in a hurry, choose method 2 or 3 instead of taking the shortcut.

## Cooking and refreezing

- **Cooking**: thawed food must be cooked through as carefully as fresh meat. Poultry is commonly advised to reach a core temperature of about 74 °C and minced meat about 71 °C. Put a thermometer in the thickest part to be sure.
- **Refreezing**: if the food was thawed in the fridge and not yet cooked, you may refreeze it. If it was thawed in water or the microwave, cook it at once; after cooking, leftovers can be frozen.
- **Once cooked**: cooked food can also be frozen, provided it was cooled and put away within about two hours.

## Checking the result

A piece that is fully thawed is evenly soft, with no ice patches in the middle, a temperature close to the fridge's and clear liquid running out. If the meat starts to smell odd or feels slimy, throw it away; but remember that spoiled food sometimes has no smell at all, so don't use smell in place of temperature and time.

Thawing liquid can contain bacteria. After you take the food out, wash your hands and wipe the tray, the sink and the nearby counter with hot soapy water. The details of avoiding bacteria spreading between raw and cooked food are in [[Cutting boards, knives and kitchen cloths: keeping them clean to avoid cross-contamination]].

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to take the food out the evening before**, then leaving it on the counter: put a reminder note on the freezer door, or use the cold-water method.
- **Soaking in an open bag**: bacteria from the water can get into the food.
- **Using lukewarm or hot water**: the outside warms very quickly.
- **Defrosting in the microwave and then leaving it**: it must be cooked at once.
- **Thawing a whole big block**: portion it into single-meal amounts when freezing, so next time you thaw only what you need.
- **Rinsing raw meat under the tap**: splashes can spread bacteria across the sink and counter; many agencies advise not rinsing raw poultry, since thorough cooking is enough.

## In short

Thawing is not just "making it soft"; it is keeping the food at a safe temperature throughout. The fridge is the best choice when you plan ahead; cold water and the microwave are options when you are in a hurry, provided you cook at once. Just avoid the habit of leaving meat on the counter and you remove the most common cause of food poisoning from frozen food.
